<aufbau-proof> — interactive proof editor

Each editor below checks its proof against a fixed theory in the browser, debounced as you type. Try breaking a proof line to see the diagnostics, then fix it to watch it go green. Once a cell has been focused, hovering a proof line shows what the compiler inferred, and Ctrl-Space completes rule names — filtered to the rules that actually apply at that line.

1. Standalone cell (theory + lemma in one element)

The theory is just axioms/terms; the cell is a self-contained lemma and only its proof body is editable.

2. A progression — later cells cite earlier ones

Cells that share a theory form one document, stitched in page order. The second cell proves main by citing the helper lemma from the first cell. Break the first cell's proof and note the second cell is not red-lined — it just stops being "verified" until the whole document is clean again.

3. A theorem cell (appends a declaration to the mm0)

A theorem cell contributes an mm0 theorem declaration and its proof, both stitched into the document. Definition cells (section 6) use the same seam.

4. Proof search (code actions)

This proof is left as an auto? hole, flagged as a warning (an unfilled hole, not a mistake). Put the caret on the auto? line and pause: a lightbulb appears at the end of the line offering the proof the search engine found. Apply it and the cell verifies.

5. A definition cell — fill in the definiens

The theory declares def reflexive: wff; with no body; the first cell supplies the definition, including a hidden dummy binder (.d: obj) the declaration never mentions. The read-only second cell proves a theorem about reflexive, so it only verifies while the definition is right. Try $ eq d d $ as the body (leaves the dummy free — rejected on the spot), or a different well-formed body (the fixed proof below stops proving it).

6. A real theory by URL (full-file mode)

A multi-theorem fixture loaded from src — the whole proof is editable, no single goal display.
